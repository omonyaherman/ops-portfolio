I archive, schedule, and publish other people’s content.

Most of my work is for creators and small teams with more posts, files, or pages than they can keep track of by hand. I build the tools that handle the repetitive part, then hand over the output, the scripts, and a note saying what I checked.

What I do

  • I pull sites, catalogues, and whole creator accounts into clean CSV, XLSX, or a folder tree that makes sense.
  • I download and file bulk video and carousels, with their captions, hashtags, and a check that every file actually opens.
  • I transcribe audio and cut long video into short clips, with subtitles as SRT or burned into the frame.
  • I schedule and publish to Instagram and Pinterest through their official APIs, and keep a record of what went out and what failed.
  • I clean, merge, and reshape spreadsheets at volume, including PDF and JSON that need to become a workbook.
  • I build static sites from structured files, deploy them, and leave the source and a README behind.

Three things I’ve built

The archive

I pull every post from a creator account, watermark-free, and file it with its caption, hashtags, an SEO title, and a hash for each slide. Every download is checked before it is recorded, so a truncated file is retried rather than marked done. The daily run catches up after a missed day instead of leaving a gap.

Publishing lanes

I keep a content library moving on Instagram and Pinterest through their official APIs, on a schedule set in advance. Each run leaves a record of what posted, what failed, and what is waiting to try again. Platform limits are built into the schedule instead of discovered when a post is rejected.

Site builds

I build static sites from structured files, so the words and images live somewhere you can read and edit. One build step turns them into pages, and one command deploys them. I hand over the source and a README, so changing a line of copy doesn’t need a CMS or me.

How a job goes

Send a written brief with the output you need, roughly how many items, and the format. If you have a sample of what the finished thing should look like, send that too.

I reply with a fixed price and a date. If something in the brief could change either, I ask before quoting, not after the work starts.

On anything beyond a small job, we start with one item as a paid sample. If it isn’t right, you stop there and you have only paid for the sample.

Then I run the batch and hand it over in one folder. If a source changes later and the script breaks, fixing it is part of the job, not a new invoice.

What you get, every time

Every delivery includes the files in the format you asked for, the script that produced them, a QA note with row counts and anything rejected or missing, and a README in plain English. I don’t ask for calls or screen shares at any point, from brief to handover.
